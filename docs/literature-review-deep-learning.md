# Deep Learning for Atmospheric Correction

Recent studies have investigated deep learning as an alternative to traditional atmospheric correction methods. These approaches learn relationships between Top-of-Atmosphere (TOA) observations and Surface Reflectance (SR) from paired or simulated data.

## Pix2Pix Atmospheric Correction

Shah et al. proposed a deep learning approach for atmospheric correction of Sentinel-2 imagery using Pix2Pix, a conditional generative adversarial network.

The model learns a direct mapping from TOA reflectance to SR using paired images. The study used 1,000 Sentinel-2 samples containing four spectral bands:

- B2 — Blue
- B3 — Green
- B4 — Red
- B8 — Near Infrared

The images were divided into `256 x 256` patches and normalized before training.

Pix2Pix uses a U-Net-based generator and a PatchGAN discriminator.

The model was evaluated using:

- SSIM
- PSNR
- RMSE
- MAE

The reported average results were:

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| SSIM | 0.961 |
| PSNR | 42.14 dB |
| RMSE | 0.0097 |
| MAE | 0.0071 |

The results indicate that image-to-image translation models can approximate atmospheric correction without explicitly receiving atmospheric parameters.

One limitation is that the validation procedure is not completely clear. The dataset is explicitly divided into training and testing subsets, while early stopping based on validation loss is also reported.

## DTACSNet

Mateo-García et al. proposed DTACSNet, a convolutional neural network for atmospheric correction and cloud detection in Sentinel-2 imagery.

The atmospheric correction branch receives Sentinel-2 Level-1C data and learns to approximate the corresponding Level-2A surface reflectance generated by Sen2Cor.

The authors therefore describe the model as a deep learning emulator of Sen2Cor rather than a model trained directly from physical ground-truth surface reflectance.

The atmospheric correction component achieved an average absolute reflectance error below 2%, with discrepancies of approximately 1.8% relative to Sen2Cor.

The study also investigated computational efficiency. DTACSNet was reported to run between 4 and 11 times faster than Sen2Cor on the tested workstation.

This work demonstrates that the Level-1C to Level-2A transformation can be approximated using a convolutional neural network while reducing computational requirements.

## Systematic Review

Shah, Raval, and Divakaran presented a systematic review of 30 peer-reviewed studies involving deep learning for atmospheric correction.

The review divides the approaches into:

- **physics-aware models**, which use atmospheric, geometric, or radiative-transfer information;
- **physics-agnostic models**, which learn a direct mapping from TOA observations to surface reflectance.

The architectures identified in the literature include:

- convolutional neural networks
- multilayer perceptrons
- autoencoders
- U-Net and R2U-Net
- vision transformers
- hybrid architectures

The review also distinguishes between pixel-level and image-level processing.

Pixel-level methods are generally computationally cheaper but do not directly model spatial relationships between neighboring pixels. Image-level models preserve spatial information but require more computational resources and larger datasets.

A recurring limitation identified in the literature is the difficulty of obtaining true surface reflectance ground truth. As a result, many models are trained using outputs from existing atmospheric correction processors or data generated through radiative transfer simulations.

## Summary

The reviewed deep learning studies indicate that atmospheric correction can be formulated as a supervised mapping between TOA observations and surface reflectance estimates.

They also show that model architecture, training data, computational cost, and the quality of the target reflectance are important factors when evaluating these approaches.
